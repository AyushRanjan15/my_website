§ What I’m reading

A modest log, kept honestly, so I’ll remember.

I read a lot more than I finish. The log is here partly to embarrass me into finishing, and partly because the best book recommendations I’ve ever gotten were one-liners from people I trusted. So: one-liners, kept honestly.

§ I

On the desk — right now

three books, slowly
  • /01
    A Philosophy of Software Design — John Ousterhout (2nd ed.)
    Rereading. The middle chapters, on layered design and the cost of shallow modules, are doing more work in my head than they did the first time.
    rereading
  • /02
    Sea of Tranquility — Emily St. John Mandel
    Bedside book. A small, structured novel that keeps doubling back on itself in a way I find restful.
    in progress
  • /03
    Compiler Construction — Niklaus Wirth
    Slowly. A tiny, opinionated book; the kind that doesn’t explain itself twice. I’m on chapter 6 and pretending I’m not lost.
    slow
§ II

Books, finished — 2026

five so far
  • b5.
    Designing Data-Intensive Applications — Martin Kleppmann
    Still the reference. I keep the chapter on consistency models taped to the inside of my brain.
    great
  • b4.
    The Practice of Programming — Kernighan & Pike
    Old, short, perfect. A book to hand to a smart intern on day one.
    classic
  • b3.
    Why We Sleep — Matthew Walker
    Useful but oversold. Read the criticisms alongside it.
    mixed
  • b2.
    The Soul of a New Machine — Tracy Kidder
    A book about engineering that’s really a book about people. I find it gets sharper every time I reread it.
    great
  • b1.
    Klara and the Sun — Kazuo Ishiguro
    Gentle, sad, very precise. Not the book about AI I expected.
    good
§ III

Papers worth your afternoon

a small, opinionated set
  • p7.
    Mixtral of Experts — Mistral AI, 2024
    The cleanest description of routing-by-expert in any paper I’ve read.
    great
  • p6.
    FlashAttention-3 — Dao et al., 2024
    The hardware model in section 4 is worth the whole paper on its own.
    great
  • p5.
    The Bitter Lesson — Rich Sutton, 2019
    Short, mostly right, occasionally weaponized in ways I don’t think Sutton intended.
    important
  • p4.
    On the Spherical Geometry of Word Embeddings — Mu & Viswanath, 2018
    Lovely small result. Embeddings have a surprising amount of structure if you take their norms seriously.
    elegant
  • p3.
    Chinchilla’s Wild Implications — nostalgebraist
    A blog post that aged into a prediction. Long. Worth it.
    essential
  • p2.
    A Note on Distributed Computing — Waldo et al., 1994
    Every five years I read this and feel slightly worse about my last design.
    classic
  • p1.
    No Silver Bullet — Fred Brooks, 1986
    An essay, technically. I reread it once a year.
    canon
